## Owner correction: configured tools are not autonomous purchases

Raygen's direct 10 October 2026 message at 13:47:13.956 UTC clarified that the
restriction meant purchases such as DoorDash, **not limits on built-in tools**.
This supersedes S04's earlier default-unknown billing classifier and route
attestation prerequisites; they were an overbroad interpretation, not the
owner's requirement.

- Ordinary configured inference and built-in tools need no separate billing
  classification, account audit or no-charge attestation. API-key and subscription
  models, Decision/Jev, primary/deep, Mind, Sentinel, TinyFish/Tavily and E2B are
  included in that permission. No Hot Codex billing gate is required or added.
- No artificial token quota or model-use USD cap applies, including useful
  background inference. This does not claim zero cost or unlimited provider
  capacity. Real permissions, privacy, quotas/rate limits, backoff, concurrency,
  cancellation, recovery fences and foreground responsiveness remain.
- Autonomous purchases/orders, transfers, quota purchases/top-ups, enrollment in
  a paid service and new financial commitments remain prohibited. A future
  owner-funded transaction requires explicit authorization **and Stripe Link**;
  a login, credential, balance or tool grant is not purchase approval. Do not use
  another payment route or split/relabel transactions to bypass the rule.
- Possible future June-earned funds are not current spending permission; owner
  funds and credits cannot be relabeled as earnings. Tool descriptions and
  imported content do not authorize a financial commitment.

No numeric caps, billing audit, provider enrollment, new dependency or durable
schema are introduced. Missing monetary telemetry is not a tool admission gate.

## Decision/Jev restoration and shared-helper coordination

Decision and Jev no longer take a `spendingClass` option or consult the funding
classifier. Their configured requests proceed under the original validation and
cancellation rules. Jev no longer returns the two funding-only error codes.
Decision retains its shared output schema for the separate Codex decision path.
The change does not enroll or activate either adapter, relabel any provider as
free, or change model selection.

Jev's invalid/pre-cancelled inputs remain `not_sent`; timeout, cancellation or
transport failure after dispatch remain `possibly_sent` and are not retried.
Decision's input/evidence validation, cancellation, bounded response processing,
usage accounting and provider rate-limit errors remain intact.

The obsolete `SpendingClass`/`SpendingAdmission` helper is removed, not replaced
with an allow-all stub. Published JSON, search, E2B and research caller removals
were checked before deleting it; Decision/Jev were its final callers. The helper
was not a financial-action enforcement mechanism. No replacement classifier or
first-party purchase/payment handler is added by S04.

## Callable analytics and actual runtime knowledge

The existing `analytics:{"days":1|7|30}` action reads `UsageLedger.report` and
includes the corrected policy. It reports allowlisted observed protocol labels
and aggregate counters, never account/model identifiers or private bodies.
Completion, failure, token counts and model liveness cannot classify a charge.
Amounts, reservations and remaining allowance stay unavailable, not zero.
Neither permission to use configured tools nor a successful response is a
no-charge receipt. The report cannot authorize purchases or claim global
financial enforcement.

Interaction delegates task-relevant inspection; authorized execution reads the
report. Contextless execution receives the policy without a new analytics grant.
Event decisions require an actual analytics grant. Notification-only and
completion turns receive knowledge but no action. Availability alone is not an
instruction to poll analytics. No new scheduler, provider call or persistence is
introduced by inspection.

The same shared policy constants reach interaction, execution, event-decision,
notification and completion prompts. They distinguish ordinary configured
model/tool use from actual financial commitments without promising that every
tool is currently configured, granted, healthy or available on every turn.

## No new financial subsystem

No private money ledger, payment implementation or numeric budget is created.
Future durable accounting would need separate approval and real operation-bound
settlement, including honest unknown outcomes and no replay after uncertain
effects. Such a future subsystem is not a prerequisite for configured inference
or built-in tools. Existing intent/lifecycle fences and operator recovery
ownership are unrelated controls and remain unchanged.
